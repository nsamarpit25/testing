If an elevator's ropes snap, what actually stops it? Not the ropes. The rails. 🛗

1854: Elisha Otis made the failure fire the brake. Rope breaks, a spring snaps straight, and catches jam into teeth on the rails. That's fail-safe.

Today it's layers:
🪢 Several steel ropes, not one, plus a counterweight
🧲 A motor brake that springs shut when the power cuts
⚙️ A governor up top: too fast, it cuts power, then locks its rope
🔩 That rope pulls wedges up against the rails. Friction stops the car
🛟 Buffers in the pit catch the rest

In 1945 a bomber hit the Empire State Building and cut car 6's ropes, governor rope included. It fell 75 floors. Betty Lou Oliver survived.

Every machine you trust has layers like this.
