# Ep 5 facts (checked 2026-10-24)
Primary source throughout: JFS Biomimicry Interview Series No.6, Eiji Nakatsu (JR West) interviewed by Japan for Sustainability, JFS Newsletter No.31, March 2005: https://www.japanfs.org/en/news/archives/news_id027795.html (below: "JFS").

1. Line 1. **400 m.** The tunnel boom was so loud that "residents 400 meters away" complained (Nakatsu, JFS). Also in Trellis (Tom McKeag, 2012, updated 2024): https://trellis.net/article/how-one-engineers-birdwatching-made-japans-bullet-train-better
2. Lines 2-4. **Mechanism.** A train entering a tunnel at speed creates a compression wave ("atmospheric pressure waves that gradually grow into waves like tidal waves", Nakatsu, JFS). The wave travels at the speed of sound and leaves the exit as a "micro-pressure wave" that makes the boom. Sources: Gigazine, 2017: https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20171110-shinkansen-500-series-biomimetics ; Trellis (above). Railway Technical Research Institute research confirms that nose shape controls how steep the entry compression wave is, and so how strong the micro-pressure wave is at the exit: Miyachi et al., RTRI Quarterly Report 64(2), 2023: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/rtriqr/64/2/64_135/_article
3. Line 5. **Nakatsu** was a JR West engineer on the 500 Series and a member of the Wild Bird Society of Japan (JFS; Trellis).
4. Line 6. **Kingfisher.** It dives from low-resistance air into high-resistance water (Nakatsu, JFS). "Barely a splash" and "800 times denser" come from Trellis. The 800x figure is not used; water/air is about 830x at 20 C.
5. Line 7. **15 m nose.** "Streamline shape that is 15m in length and almost round in cross section" (Nakatsu, JFS). Tests fired bullets of different shapes into a pipe and measured the pressure waves; the ideal shape was "almost identical to a kingfisher's beak" (JFS). Not in the narration, but usable as an on-screen beat.
6. Line 8. **Boom fades.** AskNature says the new nose "eliminated" tunnel booms (https://asknature.org/idea/shinkansen-train/, which blocks automated fetching; wording taken from a search snippet). The script softens this to "fades", because the micro-pressure wave is reduced and spread out, not removed.
7. Line 9. **10% faster, 15% less power.** The new nose cut "air pressure by 30% and electricity use by 15%" while "speeds have increased by 10% over the former series" (Nakatsu, JFS). AskNature repeats 10% / 15%. Gigazine also says 10% faster and 15% less power. **Conflict:** Trellis cites a test run showing a "13 percent reduction in the power". The script uses Nakatsu's own 15%. Also note that the power figure belongs to the whole new train (the owl-inspired pantograph and lighter body helped too), so the narration attaches it to the train, not only the nose.
8. On-screen only (optional). **300 km/h, March 1997.** The 500 Series was Japan's first train to run 300 km/h in passenger service, from March 1997 on the Sanyo Shinkansen (Shin-Osaka to Hakata): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/500_Series_Shinkansen ; Web Japan, Feb 1997: https://web-japan.org/kidsweb/archives/news/97-2/shinkansen.html
9. Not used. **Noise limit near homes.** Japan's Shinkansen environmental noise standard (Environment Agency Notice No.46, 29 July 1975) is 70 dB or less in mainly residential areas (Type I) and 75 dB in commercial/industrial areas (Type II): https://www.pref.kanagawa.jp/docs/pf7/kotsu/tokaido_shinkansen.html . This is dropped from the script: sources disagree on which limit the 500 Series was held to (Trellis says 70 dBA, Gigazine 75 dB), and there wasn't room for it.
10. Cross-check: Gemini Argon review of the draft. It confirmed lines 1-7 and 9, flagged "the boom is gone" as overstated (now "fades"), and noted that the 15% power figure is not from the nose alone (handled as in 7).
