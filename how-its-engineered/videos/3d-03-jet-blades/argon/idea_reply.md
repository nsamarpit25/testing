### 1. Fact Verification & Sourcing

1. **Turbine Entry Gas Temperature:** **1,500 to 1,700 °C** in modern large turbofans (e.g., Trent XWB / GE9X).
   * *Source:* Rolls-Royce plc (*The Jet Engine*, 5th ed., Turbine chapter; also Rolls-Royce educational resources on high-pressure turbines) and University of Cambridge DoITPoMS (Teaching and Learning Packages: *Superalloys*).
   * *Confidence:* **High.** (Rolls-Royce commonly cites "over 1,500 °C" or "around 1,600 °C" for HP turbine entry gas).
2. **Nickel-Based Superalloy Melting Point:** **~1,300 to 1,350 °C** (incipient melting of CMSX-4 / René N5 occurs around 1,320–1,380 °C, with operating limits around 1,100–1,150 °C).
   * *Source:* University of Cambridge DoITPoMS (*Superalloys* module) and ASM International (*Superalloys: A Technical Guide*).
   * *Confidence:* **High.**
3. **Gas Temperature vs. Melting Point Delta:** **~200 to 300 °C hotter** than the metal's melting point.
   * *Source:* Rolls-Royce plc (Website feature: *How to survive in the hottest part of a jet engine* / *The Jet Engine* book).
   * *Confidence:* **High.**
4. **Single-Crystal (SX) Turbine Blades Introduction:** Introduced commercially in the **early 1980s** by **Pratt & Whitney** (alloy PWA 1480 entering service in the JT9D-7R4 in 1982). They eliminate grain boundaries, which are prone to **creep, oxidation, and cracking** under high temperature and centrifugal stress.
   * *Source:* Pratt & Whitney (Company historical milestones / ASME Landmarks designation for single-crystal blades) and TMS (*The Minerals, Metals & Materials Society* proceedings on Superalloys).
   * *Confidence:* **High.**
5. **Thermal Barrier Coating (TBC) Material & Temperature Drop:** Made of **Yttria-Stabilized Zirconia (YSZ)**; lowers the underlying metal temperature by **~100 to 200 °C** (commonly cited as ~150 °C across the coating with internal cooling active).
   * *Source:* NASA Glenn Research Center ( Technical Memorandum on Thermal Barrier Coatings) and Rolls-Royce plc (*The Jet Engine*, Turbine Cooling section).
   * *Confidence:* **High.**
6. **Film-Cooling Holes per Blade:** **Several hundred** (typically **300 to 700** laser- or EDM-drilled holes depending on the engine generation).
   * *Source:* GE Aerospace (GE Reports / *The Art of Cooling* engineering articles) and Rolls-Royce plc.
   * *Confidence:* **High** (using "hundreds of tiny laser-drilled holes" in the script is 100% accurate across all modern HP blades).
7. **Scale Fact — Power Extracted & Centrifugal Load per Blade:**
   * *Power:* Each high-pressure turbine blade in a Trent-class engine extracts around **800 to 1,000 horsepower**, equivalent to **a Formula One car**.
   * *Centrifugal Load:* Spinning at over 10,000 RPM, each blade experiences a centrifugal pull of **around 10 tonnes**, equivalent to **hanging a London double-decker bus** from the blade root.
   * *Source:* Rolls-Royce plc (Official educational infographic & science outreach pages: *How does a jet engine work?* / *Trent 1000 & XWB HP Turbine facts*).
   * *Confidence:* **High.** (We will use the **Formula One car** power fact in the script as it is punchy, universally understood, and directly backed by Rolls-Royce press/educational materials).

### 5. Look & Lighting Note (Rolls-Royce / Apple Product Film Aesthetic)

* **Why Ep 2 Looked Like a Blockout vs. How Ep 3 Fixes It:**
  * *No Environment:* Zero trees, sky, ground planes, or buildings. Everything lives in an infinite dark studio void with a single high-gloss reflective floor plane (`Roughness: 0.08`, `Metallic: 0.9`, dark slate tint) that grounds the object via soft contact reflections.
  * *Geometry Polish:* Every mechanical edge on the fir-tree root, platform, and cooling holes uses a procedural Bevel modifier (`2–3 segments`, micro-radius) + Subdivision Surface so specular highlights roll smoothly across edges—never sharp CG polygons.
* **Backdrop Gradient:**
  * Radial background gradient behind the hero blade: center is deep obsidian navy (`#0B0F17`), falling off to pure pitch black (`#020305`) at the edges.
* **Studio Lighting Setup (3-Point + Accent):**
  * *Key Light:* Large overhead softbox area light (`#F4F7FA`, cool daylight white, high spread) positioned at 45° top-front to draw a clean, wide specular streak down the curved airfoil.
  * *Fill Light:* Low-intensity wide area light (`#1E293B`, slate blue) from the opposite side to keep shadow areas rich and readable without crushing detail.
  * *Rim / Kicker Lights:* Two narrow strip area lights behind the blade—one warm tungsten-amber (`#FF6B1A`) on the hot gas side, one ice blue (`#00E5FF`) on the cooling side—carving out the silhouette against the dark backdrop.
* **Material Specifications (Principled BSDF in Eevee):**
  * *Polished/Brushed Nickel Superalloy (CMSX-4):* Base Color `#8C929A`, Metallic `1.0`, Roughness `0.18`, Anisotropic `0.75` (tangent aligned vertically along the blade span, with a high-frequency procedural noise stretched 20:1 for micro-brushing).
  * *Single-Crystal Wave Pass:* Animated roughness drop from `0.28` (Voronoi grain facet normals) to `0.10` (mirror-smooth single crystal) with a golden emission edge (`#FFB800`).
  * *Ceramic Thermal Barrier Coating (YSZ):* Base Color `#EAE6DF` (matte bone/off-white), Metallic `0.0`, Roughness `0.65`, Subsurface Weight `0.15` (gives that dense, chalky sintered-ceramic look), micro-bump procedural noise for plasma-sprayed texture.
  * *Anodised Titanium Turbine Disc:* Base Color `#2A303C` (dark gunmetal), Metallic `0.95`, Roughness `0.25`.
* **Thermal & Cooling Emission Ramps (Bloom Enabled in Eevee):**
  * *Thermal Ramp (Hot Gas & Heat Map):* Black/Deep Violet (`#1A0033` at 600°C) $\rightarrow$ Crimson Red (`#CC0022` at 1,000°C) $\rightarrow$ vivid Orange (`#FF5500` at 1,300°C) $\rightarrow$ Incandescent Yellow-White (`#FFF4CC` at 1,600°C).
  * *Cooling Air Accent:* Electric Cyan (`#00F0FF`) to Deep Azure (`#0051FF`) on curve ribbons with alpha falloff along curve parameter `t`.
* **Camera & Depth of Field:**
  * Long macro focal lengths (`85mm`, `105mm`, `135mm`) flatten perspective distortion and make the 10 cm blade feel monumental.
  * Shallow Depth of Field (`f/2.0` to `f/2.8` on macro shots) with bokeh blades set to `7`, forcing the viewer's eye directly onto the laser holes, Voronoi boundaries, or fir-tree root while the rest of the blade melts into creamy specular bokeh.
