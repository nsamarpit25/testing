### 1. Fact Check (Line by Line)

*   **Line 1: "Jet engine blades run in gas up to two hundred degrees above their melting point."**
    *   *Accuracy:* **Mostly accurate, slight unit ambiguity.** Specifying "in gas" is actually *more* scientifically rigorous than the Rolls-Royce press release ("perform at temperatures..."), because it is the surrounding gas stream—not the metal itself—that reaches these temperatures. 
    *   *Flag:* Omitting "Celsius" makes the number inaccurate for US viewers thinking in Fahrenheit (200°C is a 360°F difference). Also, this only applies to high-pressure turbine blades, though "jet engine blades" is acceptable shorthand for a 30-second reel.
*   **Line 2: "Each palm-sized blade makes the power of a Formula One car."**
    *   *Accuracy:* **Accurate.** 10 cm (about 4 inches) is the exact width of an average adult palm. "Makes" is a slight simplification of "extracts from the gas stream," but it matches RR’s own wording ("generates"). Beginners will not be misled.
*   **Line 3: "Normal metal is a patchwork of tiny crystals. Heat cracks it along the seams."**
    *   *Accuracy:* **Slightly incomplete/misleading.** Grain boundary failure (creep and stress-rupture) requires **both heat and immense centrifugal pull** (the 12 tons of force in your source). Heat alone doesn’t crack an unstressed blade along its crystal grains; it’s the combination of heat softening the boundaries while centrifugal force pulls them apart. 
*   **Line 4: "So each blade is grown as one single crystal. No seams."**
    *   *Accuracy:* **Accurate.** Excellent beginner-friendly translation of single-crystal investment casting (eliminating grain boundaries).
*   **Line 5: "Inside, cool air snakes through hidden channels,"**
    *   *Accuracy:* **Accurate.** (Technically the compressor bleed air is still several hundred degrees Celsius, so it's only *relatively* cool, but "cool air" is completely fair for beginners).
*   **Line 6: "then bursts from tiny holes and blankets the surface."**
    *   *Accuracy:* **Accurate.** "Bursts" slightly dramatizes film cooling (if it bursts too hard, the air separates from the blade and fails to cool it; it actually *bleeds* out to form a boundary layer), but visually and conceptually, "blankets the surface" nails the physics for a general audience.
*   **Line 7: "On top, a ceramic coat blocks the rest of the heat."**
    *   *Accuracy:* **Overstated.** Thermal Barrier Coatings (TBCs) do not "block the rest of the heat"—they are insulators that *slow down* heat transfer and create a steep temperature drop between the gas/air film and the metal. If they blocked all remaining heat, internal cooling wouldn't need to run continuously.
*   **Line 8: "So the metal never reaches its melting point."**
    *   *Accuracy:* **Accurate.** This is the crucial distinction that resolves the paradox of Line 1.
*   **Line 9: "Follow for how the world is really engineered."**
    *   *Accuracy:* N/A (Brand sign-off).

### 2. Clarity, Flow, and TTS Performance

*   **Logic Gap (Lines 2 to 4):** Line 2 introduces massive *power/force*, and Line 1 introduces *heat*. Line 3 only blames *heat* for the cracking. Furthermore, to a beginner, "growing a single crystal" sounds like a way to raise the melting point—which makes Lines 5–8 feel slightly redundant unless you distinguish *strength* (the crystal) from *cooling* (the air and ceramic).
*   **TTS Pacing (1.3x speed):** 
    *   Line 4 ("No seams.") is a great punchy fragment, but make sure to add a period or ellipsis in the prompt so Gemini TTS doesn't rush directly into Line 5 at 1.3x speed.
    *   "Formula One" is correctly written out for TTS.

### 4. Changes

**REQUIRED CHANGES:**
1.  **Specify "Celsius" in Line 1:** Without the unit, "two hundred degrees" is factually wrong for viewers assuming Fahrenheit.
2.  **Fix "blocks the rest of the heat" in Line 7:** Change to "insulates against," "holds back," or "blocks even more heat" so you aren't claiming the ceramic is a 100% thermal barrier.

**OPTIONAL POLISH:**
1.  **Include force/pull in Line 3:** Mentioning that *heat and pull* crack the seams is more faithful to creep failure and connects directly to the power mentioned in Line 2.
2.  **Change "bursts" to "bleeds" or "streams" in Line 6:** Closer to how film cooling actually behaves aerodynamically.
