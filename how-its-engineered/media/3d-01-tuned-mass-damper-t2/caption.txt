There's a 660-tonne steel ball hanging between floors 87 and 92 of Taipei 101, and it's there on purpose.

It swings at the tower's own rhythm, so when wind shoves the building one way, the ball lags behind and pulls it back. Eight hydraulic pistons turn that motion into heat and cut the sway by up to 40%. In Typhoon Soudelor (2015) it swung a full metre, its biggest move on record.

Would you want an office on the 88th floor?
