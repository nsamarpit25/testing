How to Move a 100-Ton Rock Without a Crane

Engineering notes (numbers used on screen):
- Rock: ~35 m3 of granite (2.7 t/m3) = about 100 t.
- Rope rated 2 t vs 100 t load: 50x overload, so it snaps.
- Lever 8.16 m, pivot 16 cm from the tip: 8 m / 0.16 m = 50:1. Lifting one edge needs ~half the weight (~50 t), so ~1 t at the long end:
  the 80 kg engineer is not enough; 5 x 200 kg sandbags is. The long end drops ~1.3 m, so the tip rises 1.3/50 = 2.6 cm (force traded for distance).
  Repeated lift-and-wedge (cribbing) raises it enough to slide in 30 cm rollers.
- Sliding stone on dirt: friction ~0.6 x 100 t = 60 t. On rollers, rolling resistance ~0.02 x 100 t = 2 t.
- Pry bar ~50:1: a ~50 kg heave gives ~2.5 t, enough to nudge ~5 cm per heave.
- Capstan: 3 m arms / 15 cm drum = 20:1; rope through one pulley on the rock = 2:1; total 40x. ~50 kg push gives ~2 t pull.
  The rope winds in at 2x the rock's speed; real speed ~3.5 cm/s at walking pace (the reel shows a time-lapse).
- Rollers travel at half the load's speed, so they come out the back and new ones are picked up at the front.
