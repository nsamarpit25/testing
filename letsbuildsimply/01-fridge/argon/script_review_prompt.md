# How a block of ice becomes a refrigerator (v1)

Each line below is one beat. The Visual line describes the shot.

1. Put a block of ice in a wooden box. Congratulations, you've built a fridge.
   Visual: an ice block drops into an open wooden icebox next to a milk bottle; the lid slams.
2. But by tomorrow, the ice is a puddle, and your milk is warm again.
   Visual: the ice shrinks, water spreads across the floor, the milk turns red, and a wall clock spins.
3. So you pay for fresh ice every few days.
   Visual: ice blocks thunk in one after another while a calendar flips.
4. But in summer, the ice runs out. You need cold without ice.
   Visual: harsh sunlight through the window; the last block melts away.
5. So you run a pipe through the box, filled with a liquid that boils at minus twelve degrees.
   Visual: a coil grows inside the box and fills with blue liquid.
6. As it boils, it drags the heat out of your food. This is the evaporator.
   Visual: bubbles, and red heat particles flow from the milk into the coil. Label: EVAPORATOR.
7. But once it boils into gas, it's gone.
   Visual: gas puffs out of the open end and the coil empties.
8. So you catch the gas and squeeze it with a pump. This is the compressor.
   Visual: a black compressor drops in at the bottom back and shudders. Label: COMPRESSOR.
9. But squeezing gas makes it hot, like a bicycle pump.
   Visual: the pipe after the compressor glows orange.
10. So you zigzag that hot pipe down the back, where the heat escapes into your kitchen and the gas turns back into liquid. This is the condenser.
    Visual: a zigzag coil grows on the back and heat shimmers off it. Label: CONDENSER.
11. That's why the back of your fridge is always warm.
    Visual: camera pushes in on the warm back coil.
12. But that liquid is squeezed too hard to boil.
    Visual: a pressure gauge needle hits red.
13. So you push it through a tube thinner than spaghetti. The pressure drops, and it boils cold again.
    Visual: a hair-thin tube links the condenser to the evaporator; flow arrows run around the whole loop. Label: CAPILLARY TUBE.
14. Now the same gas goes round and round, forever.
15. But it keeps cooling until your milk freezes solid.
    Visual: the thermometer drops, frost covers the milk and the bottle cracks.
16. So a thermostat switches the pump off at four degrees, and back on when it warms up. That's the hum that comes and goes.
    Visual: a dial appears; the compressor stops and starts. Label: THERMOSTAT.
17. But every time you open the door, warm, wet air rushes in and freezes onto the pipes.
    Visual: the door swings open, orange wisps flow in, and white frost thickens on the coil.
18. So a few times a day, a heater melts it, and the water drips into a tray on the hot compressor, where it dries up.
    Visual: a red heater wire glows; drops run down a drain tube into a tray; steam rises. Label: DEFROST HEATER.
19. Close it all in a steel box with foam walls. Congratulations, you've built a fridge.
    Visual: white panels fold around everything; camera pulls back to the full fridge in the kitchen; a labeled blueprint freeze.
20. And here's the twist: it never made any cold. It just moves the heat out of your milk, and dumps it into your kitchen.
    Visual: red heat particles travel from the milk around the loop and out the back into the room.
