### 1. Fact-Check
* **Refrigerant boiling point (Beat 5):** "Minus twelve" ($-12^\circ\text{C}$) is awkward because US viewers will assume Fahrenheit, while $-12^\circ\text{C}$ doesn't cleanly match a specific common refrigerant at 1 atm (R-134a is $-26^\circ\text{C}$ / $-15^\circ\text{F}$; R-600a isobutane is $-11.7^\circ\text{C}$ / $+11^\circ\text{F}$). Saying **"minus twenty-six degrees"** or simply **"well below freezing"** avoids the C/F confusion and hits harder.
* **Condenser location (Beats 10–11):** Modern fridges usually hide the condenser coils underneath near the floor (with a fan) or bonded inside the side walls—not exposed on the back. However, the exposed black zigzag on the back is the universal visual archetype of a fridge, and "that's why the back/sides of your fridge feel warm" is still true. Keep the back coil for visual clarity, or say "back and sides."
* **Capillary tube (Beats 12–13):** Spot on. High-pressure liquid can't boil at room temperature; passing it through a ~0.5–1 mm capillary tube drops the pressure so its boiling point plummets again. (Bonus: it works exactly like an aerosol spray can getting icy cold when you spray it.)
* **Thermostat (Beat 16):** Again, $4^\circ\text{C}$ ($39^\circ\text{F}$) will confuse US viewers who think $4^\circ\text{F}$ is deep-freeze territory. Say **"just above freezing."**
* **Defrost & drain tray (Beat 18):** Very accurate. Defrost heaters run 1–3 times a day for ~20 minutes, draining onto a pan atop the hot compressor/condenser loop where the water evaporates into the room.
* **Frost blocking cooling (Beat 17):** You skipped *why* frost on the pipes is a problem. Ice is actually a thermal insulator—once the coil crusts over in frost, it acts like a blanket and stops absorbing heat from the box. You need that failure beat to justify the heater.

### 2. Retention & Causal Chain Review
* **The Hook (Beats 1–2):** Good, though "milk turns red" isn't a physical failure—make the milk curdle into chunky green sludge and pop its cap off.
* **Dead Zone 1 — Beats 3–4 (The Ice Delivery):** This is a swipe risk. Buying more ice isn't an *engineering* iteration on the box; it's a historical tangent. Cut Beats 3 and 4 entirely. Go straight from "the ice melts" to "you need something that gets cold without melting away."
* **Dead Zone 2 — Beat 12 ("Squeezed too hard to boil"):** This is abstract physics that breaks the visual chain. Instead, frame the failure around *temperature*: the condenser cooled the liquid down to room temperature, **but room-temperature liquid won't cool your milk.** *So* you choke it through a tiny tube (like a spray can nozzle) so the pressure drops and it flashes freezing cold again.
* **Missing Failure — Beat 17:** As noted above, frost forming on pipes looks like *extra* cold to a layperson! You have to show that thick frost insulates the pipe so the fridge gets warm again.
