### 5. Fact Sheet (100% Technical Accuracy)

1. **Phase-Change Cooling (Latent Heat of Vaporization):**
   * *Claim:* Evaporating/boiling liquid absorbs heat from its surroundings (skin or milk).
   * *Fact:* Phase transition from liquid to gas requires significant thermal energy (latent heat of vaporization), drawing heat out of the adjacent medium.
2. **Low-Boiling-Point Refrigerant:**
   * *Claim:* The pipe is filled with a liquid that boils below freezing.
   * *Fact:* Modern domestic refrigerators primarily use **R-600a (isobutane)**, which boils at **-11.7°C (+10.9°F)** at 1 atm, or **R-134a (tetrafluoroethane)**, which boils at **-26.3°C (-15.3°F)** at 1 atm. Both boil well below the freezing point of water (0°C / 32°F). Avoiding a raw un-united number in the voiceover prevents °C/°F confusion while remaining 100% accurate.
3. **Vapor-Compression Heat Pump Cycle:**
   * *Claim:* Compressor squeezes gas, making it hot and high-pressure; condenser dumps heat into the kitchen, condensing gas back into a high-pressure warm liquid.
   * *Fact:* Compressing a gas increases both its pressure and temperature ($PV = nRT$ / adiabatic compression) above ambient room temperature (~20–25°C / 68–77°F) so heat flows naturally from the condenser coils into the cooler kitchen air, allowing the refrigerant to condense while still at high pressure.
4. **Warm Fridge Walls:**
   * *Claim:* The sides of a modern fridge feel warm because the condenser coils are hidden there.
   * *Fact:* While older fridges had exposed black condenser grids on the back, most modern residential refrigerators route the condenser tubing just inside the outer sheet-metal cabinet walls (skin condensers) to radiate heat out through the sides.
5. **Capillary Tube (Joule–Thomson / Adiabatic Flash Evaporation):**
   * *Claim:* Forcing high-pressure liquid through a tube thinner than spaghetti drops the pressure, turning it freezing cold again.
   * *Fact:* Domestic fridge capillary tubes have an internal diameter of **0.5 mm to 1.5 mm** (a standard dry spaghetti strand is ~1.8–1.9 mm thick). The narrow bore creates a massive pressure drop; as the liquid exits into the wider low-pressure evaporator, a portion of it instantly flash-evaporates, dropping the mixture's temperature well below freezing.
6. **Thermostat Setpoint:**
   * *Claim:* A sensor cuts the power right at **37 degrees Fahrenheit**.
   * *Fact:* The FDA and appliance manufacturers recommend an ideal fresh-food compartment temperature of **37°F (2.8°C)**—safely above water's freezing point (32°F / 0°C) so milk won't freeze, and below the bacterial danger zone (40°F / 4.4°C).
7. **Thermodynamic Law ("Never Made Cold"):**
   * *Claim:* A refrigerator never makes cold; it pumps heat from inside the box into the kitchen.
   * *Fact:* "Cold" is simply the absence of heat. A refrigerator is a closed-loop heat pump that moves thermal energy from a low-temperature reservoir (the fridge interior) to a high-temperature reservoir (the kitchen).
