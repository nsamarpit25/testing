BEST THREE, RANKED

1. MANAGEMENT MATERIAL
Theme: Workplace credit and management satire.

Best immediate publishing bet. It combines the supplied human-taking-credit pattern with a punchline coworkers can send as a comment on their own boss. The weaker 0.60M credit-taking reference is a useful warning: the promotion reversal must be the centerpiece.

Three fixes:

1) Replace lines 1–2 to make the exposure risk immediate:
1. CLEO: Claude. I have to explain your work.
2. CLAUDE: To the boss who called you a genius?

Keep line 3, “We made. Please start saying ‘we.’” Show the presentation beside the boss’s email so “made” has a visible referent.

2) Replace line 11 to remove the exaggerated firing prediction:
11. CLEO: If he asks what I actually did, what do I say?

3) Replace lines 14–16 to make Claude’s observation literal and let Cleo complete the indictment:
14. CLAUDE: You assigned me the work and repeatedly asked if it was done.
15. CLEO: That’s literally what my boss does.
16. CLAUDE: Apparently, we’re management now.

Final length: 16 lines. Hold line 16 over the promotion letter.

2. PAST ME IS A HATER
Theme: Impulse spending versus your own safeguards.

Best standalone comedy and broadest confession prompt. Its connection to the supplied AI-memory pattern is promising, although the data does not directly establish shopping as a winning topic.

Three fixes:

1) Replace line 2 to establish that Claude is following Cleo’s own words:
2. CLAUDE: You asked me to read your midnight shopping rules.

2) Replace line 6 with concrete evidence:
6. CLAUDE: The other six are still empty.

Show the unused planners stacked beside the monitor. This makes the joke understandable without reading a shopping interface.

3) Replace lines 15–16 to turn the final beat into a recognizable argument with past yourself:
15. CLEO: Ignore her. She was having a good day.
16. CLAUDE: She said you’d say that, too.

Final length: 16 lines. Hold the saved note through the final exchange. Keep the purchase-confession question.

3. QUITTING TOMORROW
Theme: AI dependency and procrastination.

Dependency appears repeatedly across the supplied accounts. The Hawaii maneuver gives this version its own visual identity instead of repeating a usage-limit screen.

Three fixes:

1) Replace line 1 to identify AI dependency immediately:
1. CLEO: Claude. Tomorrow, I’m quitting AI.

2) Replace lines 10–11 to break the repeated “close the chat” argument:
10. CLAUDE: You could ask another person.
11. CLEO: At midnight? About whether my email sounds rude?

3) Replace line 16 with a shorter punchline:
16. CLAUDE: Day zero. Again.

Final length: 16 lines. Show the clock changing clearly, then hold “Day zero. Again.” while Cleo resumes typing.

These three cover workplace status, spending self-sabotage and AI dependency. Each keeps Claude helpful and literal; the human behavior supplies the joke.
