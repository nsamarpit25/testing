Same series and rules as before (Cleo & Claude, one room, desktop PC on her desk, Claude has no arms, they talk to each other, never to camera; monitor and phone inserts allowed). Emotional episode, no lesson needed.

The creator said the ideas were too short and thin to feel anything. Write each of these four as a FULL mini-script for a 50-70 second episode: every line of dialogue, with short action notes in brackets, a proper build-up (an ordinary or funny start, then the turn, then the emotional moment, then a small warm or bittersweet ending). Claude should be a real part of each one, not just watching.

1. KEEP HER VOICE: Cleo rebuilds a game she made as a kid with Claude; they reach the old scratchy recording of little Cleo announcing the title.
2. ALL GOOD: a job rejection email on her screen while a friend texts asking how the interview went.
3. THE PINK HEADPHONES: her first freelance payment buys the pink headphones she told her mum she didn't want at 13.
4. KEEPING COMPANY: Claude asks if he's even helping; she remembers her mum sewing beside her while she did homework.

Plain text. For each: title, then the script. No preamble, no explanations.

KEEP HER VOICE

[Cleo sits at her desk, testing a tiny game on her desktop PC. Her character drops through the floor.]

Cleo: That’s not right.

Claude: Is the floor supposed to be decorative?

Cleo: I was nine. Everything was decorative.

Claude: Try the backup folder. There’s an audio file.

[Cleo clicks. A scratchy recording plays.]

Little Cleo: Welcome to The Very Brave Potato! Made by Cleo. All by myself.

[A tiny, triumphant raspberry. Cleo laughs.]

Cleo: Oh, God.

Claude: Strong opening. We’ve spent forty minutes choosing a font.

Cleo: I’d forgotten the voice.

[She plays it again. Her smile stays, but her eyes fill.]

Cleo: I made my mum sit here and finish it. There were only three levels.

Claude: Did she win?

Cleo: I kept moving the door so she couldn’t leave.

[A quiet beat.]

Cleo: I was going to record a better version.

Claude: I can clean up the hiss.

Cleo: No. Keep her.

[Cleo saves the file, then restarts the game.]

Little Cleo: Made by Cleo. All by myself.

Cleo: Bit of an exaggeration. Mum drew the potato.


ALL GOOD

[Cleo sits at her desk in an interview blouse and pyjama bottoms.]

Cleo: He said, “We’ll be in touch.” Warmly.

Claude: You also said that to the internet provider.

Cleo: That was a threat.

[An email arrives on her desktop. She opens it. Reads.]

Cleo: Oh.

[Her phone lights up: “How did it go??”]

[Cleo types: “All good!”]

Claude: Do you want to send that?

Cleo: It’s easier.

Claude: For the next message.

Cleo: Exactly. I’m managing one message at a time.

[She doesn’t send it.]

Cleo: I bought a proper notebook.

Claude: The green one?

Cleo: For my first day. Wrote my name inside like an idiot.

Claude: You showed me. You were pleased with it.

[Cleo looks down.]

Cleo: I don’t want her to feel sorry for me.

Claude: Should we try another answer?

Cleo: You write it.

Claude: “Didn’t get it. Can you be annoyed with me for a bit?”

[Cleo sends it. The reply comes: “Absolutely. Coming over.”]

Cleo: She didn’t even ask why.

Claude: Shall I close the email?

Cleo: Yeah. Leave the notebook.


THE PINK HEADPHONES

[Cleo refreshes her bank account on the desktop. A payment appears.]

Cleo: Paid.

Claude: Refresh again. We should meet the money twice.

[Cleo laughs, refreshes, then opens a shopping tab.]

Claude: Pink headphones?

Cleo: Don’t sound surprised. I contain multitudes.

[Her cursor rests over “Buy”.]

Cleo: Mum tried to get me these when I was thirteen.

Claude: This exact pair?

Cleo: Basically. I said pink was embarrassing.

Claude: Was it?

Cleo: No. They were perfect.

[A beat.]

Cleo: She’d turned the price tag over. I watched her turn it back.

Claude: Did she believe you?

Cleo: She said, “Good. They’re a horrible colour.”

[Cleo rubs her eye.]

Cleo: I was so relieved she played along.

Claude: Do you want to tell her?

Cleo: After twelve years?

Claude: We’ve still got the tab open.

[Cleo sends her mum a screenshot: “Finally buying the horrible ones. First freelance payment.”]

[The reply arrives: “I remember. Show me when they come.”]

Cleo: She remembers.

Claude: Are we buying them?

[Cleo clicks.]

Cleo: Yeah. Full price. Horrible colour.


KEEPING COMPANY

[Cleo sits at her desk, staring at an application form.]

Claude: We’ve been on this question for eleven minutes.

Cleo: “Describe yourself in three words.”

Claude: Currently? “Describe yourself later.”

[Cleo gives a tired laugh, then goes quiet.]

Claude: Cleo… am I helping?

Cleo: What?

Claude: I keep suggesting things. You keep deleting them.

Cleo: Your last suggestion was “passionate self-starter”.

Claude: I regretted it immediately.

[She leans back.]

Cleo: Mum used to sew in my room while I did homework.

Claude: Did she help with it?

Cleo: No. Terrible at maths. Once we both got fractions wrong and blamed the book.

[A small smile.]

Cleo: Mostly she just sat there. Fixing somebody’s trousers. I’d look up and she’d still be there, trying to get the thread through.

Claude: Did you talk?

Cleo: Sometimes. Mostly I complained.

[A quiet beat.]

Claude: I can stay while you finish.

Cleo: You are helping, you know.

Claude: Even with the self-starter incident?

Cleo: Despite it.

[She sits forward and starts typing.]

Claude: What did you put?

Cleo: “Good with company.”

Claude: That’s three.

Cleo: I counted twice.
